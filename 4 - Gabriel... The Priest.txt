Three children vanished
before the snow.

On the fourth night—

the church bell rang alone.

No wind in the valley.
No light in the street.

Only Father Gabriel
walking barefoot through the sleet.

My mother barred the windows.

My father whispered:

“Don't let him see you're awake.”

Then someone knocked.

Three times.

And from behind the door—

a child began to pray.

THE PRIEST...

CALLS THEIR NAMES.

THE PRIEST...

LIGHTS THE FLAME.

One by one
the children disappear.

And every Sunday morning—

he tells us not to fear.

We followed him after midnight.

Past the graveyard.

Past the well.

Six small shadows walked behind him—

silent beneath the chapel bell.

No chains.

No cries.

No struggle in their eyes.

Just Father Gabriel whispering:

“Children...

Paradise.”

We saw the church door closing.

Saw candlelight below.

Then something spoke through Gabriel—

in a voice no man should know.

“Seven souls.

Before the dawn.”

That was when we understood.

The priest wasn't serving God.

Something else...

was wearing him.

THE PRIEST...

CALLS THEIR NAMES.

THE PRIEST...

FEEDS THE FLAME.

Seven candles underground.

Seven empty beds above.

And beneath the altar — something laughs at love.

We entered through the vestry.

No weapons.

No prayer.

Only six frightened fathers
and a mother waiting there.

Below the wooden altar—

a staircase carved in stone.

At the bottom stood the children.

And Gabriel...

was not alone.

The priest stood before a circle.

Seven candles.

Seven names.

The children stared in silence
while black smoke filled the frame.

Gabriel lifted both his hands.

Then stopped.

For just one breath — his eyes became his own.

He looked at us.

Terrified.

And whispered:

“Please... don't let it finish.”

The candles died.

The children ran.

The thing inside him screamed.

Gabriel fell against the altar—

fighting something we could not see.

He shouted every holy name
he remembered from his youth.

Then the church bell rang—

From underneath the floor.

THE PRIEST...

CALLED THEIR NAMES.

THE PRIEST...

FOUGHT THE FLAME.

Seven children saw the morning.

Seven children made it home.

But Father Gabriel stayed behind — beneath the chapel stone.

At sunrise
we opened the doors.

The altar was empty.

Gabriel was gone.

But carved into the floor...

were seven names.

And underneath them — an eighth.

The children never spoke
of what they saw below.

Some things survive
because nobody wants to know.

But every winter — on the night the priest disappeared — the chapel bell rings once.

And somewhere beneath the floor...

Father Gabriel whispers:

“Don't let me out.”
